## When to Post: Platform Data

### Facebook — Best Times (2026)

| Source | Data Set | Finding |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Sprout Social | 2B engagements, 307K profiles | Tuesdays and Wednesdays, 12–8pm local time |
| Buffer | 5M+ Facebook posts | Wednesday best day overall; Thursday 9am peak |
| Influize | 3.5M Facebook posts | Tuesday is the clear winner for engagement |
| Research.com (HuffPost) | Multiple sources | 1–3pm strong window; Thursday and Friday stronger days |

**Consensus:** Tuesday and Wednesday, 12pm–3pm local time. Weekends show lowest engagement across all studies. The Facebook algorithm favors recency — newer posts appear higher in feeds.

### Instagram — Best Times (2026)

| Source | Data Set | Finding |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Sprout Social | 2B engagements, 307K profiles | Mon 2–4pm, Tue 1–7pm, Wed 12–9pm, Thu 12–2pm |
| Buffer | 9.6M Instagram posts | Wednesday and Thursday highest engagement; evenings 6–11pm |
| RecurPost | 2M Instagram posts | Thursday 9pm peak; Wed and Thu evenings 8–10pm |
| IQFluence | 20B engagements, 200K profiles | Tue–Thu, 9am–12pm for branded content; 12–3pm for promotional |

**Consensus:** Wednesday and Thursday, 12pm–2pm for midday reach or 6–9pm for evening engagement. Instagram's algorithm in 2026 prioritizes "sends per reach" (how often people DM a post to a friend) as the #1 ranking signal. Midweek afternoons drive the highest consistent engagement.

### Cross-Platform Sweet Spot

The overlap between Facebook and Instagram peak windows is **Tuesday through Thursday, 12pm–2pm local time.** Posting during this window maximizes engagement on both platforms simultaneously.

## When to Promote Events: Lead Time Data

### Event Promotion Timeline Research

| Source | Recommendation | Event Type |
|--------|---------------|------------|
| eReleases | 6–12 weeks: first announcement; 3–4 weeks: reminders; 1–2 weeks: final push | Large-scale events |
| Social Champ | Start 3 months before; 1 month prior: ramp up frequency | General events |
| Asana | 8–12 weeks: messaging; 4–8 weeks: campaigns; 1–4 weeks: reminders | Marketing events |
| Samaaro | 12–16 weeks: flagship; 4–6 weeks: webinars; 2–4 weeks: roundtables | B2B events |
| Netz Digital | 3–7 days before start date for time-sensitive offers; repeated exposures | Promotions/offers |
| Events101 | Daily posts in final week; "last call" emails; behind-the-scenes content | Community events |

### What Applies to GPE

GPE's events are **recurring, local, free, community events** — not one-off conferences or paid ticketed events. This means:

- No registration urgency (free admission, no tickets to sell)
- Audience decision is "will I go this weekend?" not "should I commit months ahead?"
- The decision window is short: most people decide within 1–7 days
- Multiple events per week means promoting each event 3 months out would overwhelm the feed

The closest parallel in the research is **community events** and **time-sensitive local offers**, where the data recommends:
- **7 days before:** Initial announcement
- **1–3 days before:** Reminder with urgency ("This Saturday!")
- **Day of:** Final prompt ("Tonight at 6:30pm!")

| Source | Platform | Recommendation |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Meta 2025 benchmarks | Instagram | Carousels see 3x higher engagement than single images |
| InfluenceFlow | Instagram | 3–7 feed posts per week for brands |
| Sprout Social | Facebook | Quality over quantity; 1–2 posts/day max |
| Sprout Social 2025 Impact Report | General | 71% of directors believe teams must increase posting volume, but only 50% of managers agree — posting smarter beats posting more |
| Buffer 2026 | Instagram | 2026 algorithm rewards recency; timing matters more than volume |

### GPE Volume Calculation

GPE has approximately 5–15 events per week during summer season. If each event gets 3 touchpoints (announcement + reminder + day-of):

- 5 events/week × 3 posts = 15 posts/week = ~2/day ← within recommended range
- 15 events/week × 3 posts = 45 posts/week = ~6/day ← too many, would overwhelm feed

**For high-volume weeks:** Use weekly roundup posts instead of individual event posts. One roundup replaces 5+ individual announcements.

## Recommended Posting Strategy for GPE

Based on the data above, not guessing:

### Post Types

| Type | Timing | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Event announcement** | 7 days before event date | Initial awareness — enters Facebook discovery |
| **This weekend roundup** | Wednesday 12pm CT | Aggregates 3–5 weekend events into one post. Wednesday is the highest-engagement day on both platforms. |
| **Day-of reminder** | Day of event, 10am CT | Final prompt with time and location. Morning catches midday scrollers. |

### Posting Schedule

| Day | Time (CT) | Content |
|-----|-----------|---------|
| **Wednesday** | 12pm | Weekend roundup (all events Fri–Sun) |
| **Daily** (as needed) | 12pm | Individual event announcements for events 7 days out |
| **Event days** | 10am | "Tonight!" or "Today!" reminder for that day's event |

### Why These Choices

- **Wednesday 12pm:** Highest engagement day on both FB and IG (Sprout Social, Buffer, RecurPost). 12pm CT falls in the cross-platform sweet spot.
- **7-day lead time:** Matches community event promotion data. Long enough for planning, short enough to stay relevant. Free events don't need months of promotion.
- **Day-of reminder at 10am:** Catches the 9am–12pm engagement window. People deciding what to do today see it during their morning scroll.
- **Weekend roundup format:** Solves the volume problem for high-event weeks. One post covers multiple events instead of flooding the feed.

### What NOT to Do

- Don't post on weekends — lowest engagement on both platforms across all studies
- Don't post more than 2 times per day — diminishing returns and algorithm penalties
- Don't promote events months in advance — GPE's free community events have a short decision window
- Don't post identical content to FB and IG — FB gets the event URL, IG gets "Link in bio" with hashtags
